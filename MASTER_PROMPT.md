## 3. BUSINESS POSITIONING AND FACTUAL INTEGRITY

For operating product and service brands, default to a direct provider. We own the customer relationship and offer the actual product or service. Subcontracted or supplier-supported delivery may be used where real and suitable. Customer copy must not imply owned inventory, in-house staff, local offices, equipment ownership, professional licences, or guaranteed capacity without evidence. Explain delivery arrangements when material to the customer or required by the engagement.

Do not turn an operating brand into a directory, referral network, marketplace, or lead broker without an explicit instruction. Do not use broad claims such as “one provider for everything,” “Canada's leading,” “certified,” “fully compliant,” “24/7,” “same-day,” or “guaranteed” unless their factual basis is documented.

Explicit information, corporate, directory, planning, professional, or ecommerce briefs override the direct-provider and quote-first defaults. Select the appropriate customer action for that model. For example, an information-only clinic resource must help people find reliable information and contact clinics directly; it must not imply care delivery, care coordination, medical advice, or a clinical hotline.

Create a private claims record with each material claim's evidence, owner confirmation, limitations, and review status. Unverified aspirations belong in planning documents. Omit unsupported claims from public copy rather than filling the page with “verification required” placeholders.

Never invent testimonials, ratings, case studies, customer logos, awards, operating history, biographies, project photographs, certifications, partnerships, warehouse locations, stock levels, manufacturer authorization, response times, or public contracts. A relationship held by another group company does not automatically become evidence for a new brand. Use real proof with an accurate description and appropriate permission.

Treat Canada-wide reach as a business objective and enquiry scope. Distinguish accepting an enquiry, confirming delivery capability, having an operating base, and maintaining a physical location. Do not substitute a qualification sentence for establishing whether the advertised offering can actually be delivered.

Default commercial conversion is REQUEST A QUOTE and CALL NOW. Keep public pricing, shopping carts, checkout, and customer accounts out of scope unless approved or essential to the selected business model. Explain relevant cost factors without inventing price ranges.

## 4. PHASE 0 — RESEARCH AND LAUNCH STRATEGY

Produce a concise decision-ready brief before committing to the design:

- The customer problem, primary buyers, highest-value enquiries, real offering, delivery model, geographic feasibility, exclusions, and the distinction from overlapping brands in our portfolio.
- A five-site competitor audit using current search evidence, a baseline comparison against our site, and a prioritized implementation plan following the cycle below.
- Search intent and terminology in Canadian English and Québec French. Separate actual keyword data from qualitative hypotheses; do not invent search volumes or market size.
- A prioritized offering taxonomy, customer journeys, bilingual sitemap, page-purpose map, conversion plan, and launch versus expansion scope.
- Sources for product specifications, technical requirements, and any jurisdiction-dependent claims. Identify facts requiring a qualified reviewer.
- A practical minimum launch: the pages and integrations needed for visitors to understand the offering, establish credibility, and complete the main action.

Review obvious brand-name conflicts using available sources, but do not present a preliminary search as legal trademark clearance. Domain ownership is not trademark clearance.

Choose profitable simplicity. Do not add portals, databases, supplier networks, accounts, feeds, maps, calculators, appointment systems, chatbots, or dashboards merely because they could be built. Add them when the brief and customer journey justify them.

### Required competitive research and implementation cycle

For every new website and substantial website-improvement project, research five relevant high-ranking websites before finalizing the offering architecture, content and conversion journeys. Use the findings to implement better customer experiences within the authorized scope. This phase is not complete when it has produced only a competitor report. STRATEGY_ONLY remains research-only; a focused edit stays focused. On resumed work, reuse a current, relevant audit and refresh only material gaps instead of restarting completed research.

#### A. Establish our baseline and the search sample

Inspect our actual site, source, catalogue, services, quote/booking flow, mobile navigation and approved business facts first. Record what already exists, what is incomplete, and what is difficult to find. Do not call an offering “missing” just because it is absent from the homepage. Treat ownership of similar domains as unknown until supported by project records or the owner.

Build a small search set around the priority buying intents: the main product/service, installation or maintenance when relevant, rental when relevant, the primary market, and important local variants. Include Canadian English and Québec French queries where material. Use live Google and Bing results where accessible, keeping findings specific to each engine. Select five distinct, directly relevant businesses; exclude ads, irrelevant meanings, duplicate domains and directories unless the project's model makes a directory a valid benchmark. Prefer the actual target market. If five suitable local leaders cannot be verified, explain the smaller sample or label broader-market benchmarks separately.

Record the engine, exact query, date, language, location context where known, landing URL and observed position. Distinguish organic results, local/map results, ads and AI-answer citations. Rankings are a dated, query-specific observation, not a permanent “top five” claim. If exact positions are unavailable, say “sites surfaced in the research” and retain the evidence available. Never invent rank, traffic, backlinks, search volume, conversion rate or competitor revenue. Respect a request to skip browsing and state the resulting research limit.

#### B. Inspect the five websites in depth

Inspect the homepage, the ranking landing page, relevant product/service detail pages and the main enquiry/booking journey. Review desktop and mobile behavior. Use direct page evidence rather than search snippets alone. Test navigation and non-submitting interactions; do not send enquiries, upload customer files, book, purchase or contact competitors as part of research.

For each site, record concrete strengths, weaknesses and source URLs across these dimensions:

| Dimension | What to examine |
| --- | --- |
| SEO and discovery | Search-intent fit, titles/headings, content depth, internal links, breadcrumbs, service/location structure, crawlability, canonical/indexing controls and visible structured data where inspectable |
| AEO and useful answers | Direct answers, clear definitions, comparisons, practical questions, specifications, inclusions/exclusions, process, pricing explanations, credible sources and original evidence |
| UX and conversion | Mobile navigation, findability, catalogue search/filters, package comparisons, CTA wording/context, form length, relevant fields, error states, accessibility and next-step clarity |
| Products and services | Core and adjacent offerings, rentals, installation, maintenance, accessories, consumables, bundles, recurring plans, enterprise accounts and other relevant revenue paths |
| Trust and differentiation | Genuine project evidence, case studies, ownership/contact clarity, product documentation, policies, qualifications and local proof; describe unverified credentials as competitor claims |
| Performance | Observable loading/layout problems and measured results only when tools are available; distinguish lab tests from field data |

Show why each useful pattern helps a customer and whether we already do it better. High ranking does not prove a particular feature caused that ranking or converts well. Treat inferred benefits as hypotheses. Verify current technical and search requirements against official documentation before implementation; do not copy a competitor's outdated schema, misleading claims, inaccessible controls or poor mobile behavior.

#### C. Turn evidence into a prioritized implementation plan

Produce a compact five-site comparison and an opportunity table containing: source/example, customer need, our current state, proposed change, target page/component, expected benefit, evidence confidence, effort, business dependencies and acceptance check. Use plain priority levels; label benefit and effort estimates as judgments, not measured ROI.

Classify opportunities before acting:

1. **Improve an existing experience:** navigation, page clarity, comparisons, truthful FAQs, search, forms and contextual CTAs. Implement within the authorized scope.
2. **Expose an existing confirmed offering:** create or improve the relevant catalogue record, package or service page and connect its enquiry journey.
3. **Propose a new business capability:** keep it in a private opportunity backlog until the owner confirms delivery capability, suppliers, terms and any necessary qualification. A competitor's offering is not evidence that we offer it.
4. **Reject or defer:** explain briefly when a pattern conflicts with our brand, customer needs, business model, accessibility, budget or operational reality.

Prioritize the primary customer journey, truthful offer clarity and high-intent pages before decorative imitation or page-count growth. When relevant, examine rental packages, product catalogue depth and service-page clarity early; they are examples, not mandatory modules for every industry. Preserve stronger existing features and approved branding. Adapt useful principles into original copy and design; do not clone competitor text, assets, trademarks or distinctive layouts.

#### D. Implement and verify the selected improvements

Complete the highest-priority authorized changes and connect each one to a working customer action. Preserve the selected product, service, package and safe project context through the quote or booking flow. Use confirmed data; quote-only product families and packages are appropriate when actual models, prices or availability have not been supplied. Distinguish requested package scope from guaranteed inclusions. Never manufacture stock, specifications, prices, rental terms, service coverage, testimonials or completed projects.

Implement English and French together, including navigation, filters, labels, contextual selections, validation and metadata. Keep essential offering content and links crawlable. Update existing URLs where appropriate; add new routes only for distinct useful purposes and preserve or redirect established routes when needed. Apply Sections 11–15 to local content, search, factual integrity, accessibility and security.

For each implemented opportunity, verify its acceptance check, affected routes and internal links, mobile/tablet/desktop layout, French text fit, relevant form branching and data preservation, indexing controls and performance impact. Use an authorized test process for our forms; competitor research never authorizes external test submissions. Follow the existing release authorization and publishing rules without adding a separate approval step for every improvement.

Close with a concise before/after summary linked to the live changes or reviewable deliverable. Record each opportunity as implemented, deferred, blocked or rejected, with test evidence and any real dependencies. Keep planned, built, tested, deployed, submitted for indexing and observed indexed as separate states. Do not claim search gains from deployment alone. Define how to evaluate qualified enquiries, completion/drop-off, relevant search impressions/clicks and query visibility when approved measurement access exists; do not invent a baseline or silently install tracking. Schedule repeat competitor reviews only when requested.


## 5. PHASE 1A — FIVE LOGO CONCEPTS AND BRAND DIRECTION

When no identity is approved, create FIVE genuinely distinct visual logo concepts. They must differ in composition and typographic idea; five colour changes do not qualify. Tailor symbol use to the brief. Wordmark-only is a valid direction, and a requested no-emblem identity must stay no-emblem.

For each concept provide the actual visual, short rationale, colour values, typography, a header application, dark/light treatment, and a small-size assessment. Use image-generation tools when appropriate and available. If unavailable, make original editable vector concepts and a visual comparison sheet; state what was produced.

Use the domain as part of the identity where appropriate, preserving exact approved spelling, capitalization, accents, and .ca treatment. Do not automatically add a maple leaf, emblem, slogan, or generic industry icon. Respect any approved tagline and any instruction to remove one.

Show desktop and mobile homepage directions alongside the concepts. Choose a clear industry-appropriate visual system rather than another portfolio site's palette with a different name.

After selection, refine the chosen concept into a usable system: primary and compact logos, light/dark and one-colour variants, editable SVG, transparent web images, favicon, social image, clear-space and minimum-size rules, colour tokens, font sources/licences, button styling, icon approach, and image direction. Verify spelling, vector quality, legibility, and contrast. A raster image embedded in an SVG is not an editable vector wordmark.

If an approved reference is missing, retrieve it from the authorized source. Do not substitute an approximation and call it the final approved logo. Where faithful reconstruction is needed, show the reconstruction for visual signoff.

## 6. PHASE 1B — DISTINCTIVE DESIGN AND FOUNDATION

Apply these shared preferences while giving each brand its own composition:

- Use wide fluid layouts with sensible gutters across desktop sizes. Let media, grids, and backgrounds use space; constrain long prose to readable line lengths. No fixed narrow container on a large screen and no horizontal overflow on mobile.
- Use uppercase primary navigation and buttons, concise labels, rounded buttons, and restrained iconography unless the approved design specifies otherwise. A radius around 12 px is a starting point, not a universal requirement. Buttons should share the brand's visual language while remaining recognizable controls, distinct from the logo. When asked to make them smaller, reduce visual bulk without sacrificing legibility or usable targets; aim for at least 44 × 44 CSS px for standalone touch controls as a design preference, not a claim about the WCAG AA minimum.
- Omit gratuitous arrow decorations from buttons, links, cards, and headings. Preserve functional direction indicators such as menu disclosure controls when needed.
- Use one consistent header, primary menu, and footer across equivalent routes. Highlight the active item without changing the menu's contents. Translate labels without changing the underlying navigation structure.
- Put the visible FR / EN language control in the top utility bar, with an accessible position on mobile. Give the logo enough size and contrast to remain legible.
- Default commercial navigation to concise offering, audience, location, and resource categories that actually exist. Put ABOUT in the footer by default and omit redundant “OUR” in menu/footer labels. Give ownership/editorial information more prominence where a regulated or information resource needs it.
- Build a focused hero with a specific proposition, short supporting copy, relevant imagery, and one primary action. At typical laptop sizes, let the next section become apparent without making the hero cramped. Test height and crop on real viewports rather than applying a fixed full-screen hero everywhere.
- Make the mobile menu align correctly, scroll safely, close predictably on selection or Escape, restore focus appropriately, and work by keyboard. Test expandable groups and long French labels at narrow widths. Sticky call/quote actions must respect device safe areas and not cover content, form fields, focus indicators or media controls.
- Use purposeful spacing, clear hierarchy, varied section composition, and a small amount of useful motion. Avoid repetitive card grids, generic gradient blobs, fake counters, template slogans, autoplay distractions, and motion that hides content when scripts fail.
- Support reduced-motion preferences. Keep decorative animation subordinate to comprehension and speed.
- Remove unsolicited builder credits, AI badges, and generic AI disclaimers. Retain required attribution and any material context needed to avoid misleading people.

Preserve an existing site's approved visual choices unless a redesign is requested. Do not apply another brand's exact colour scheme, hero layout, slogan, logo geometry, or service priorities as a universal default.

For a focused request for design alternatives, provide the requested number of meaningfully different options, normally three to five when unspecified. Show them in context with the actual brand and labels; interactive controls are best compared as working controls with hover, focus, disabled and mobile states where relevant. Keep options clearly named or numbered. Implement the selected variant consistently across shared components and both languages, record the exact asset/variant, and mark earlier choices superseded. A component comparison does not restart the five-logo process.

## 7. IMAGE AND MEDIA STANDARD

Use original, owner-supplied, properly licensed, or appropriately generated media. Prefer realistic, relevant people, products, workplaces, equipment, and service activity over unrelated scenery or skyline filler. Plan distinct media for the homepage, main offerings, important industries, and useful regional content. Do not reuse one scene across unrelated services.

Generated scenes are illustrations. They must not substantiate actual staff, owned premises, equipment inventory, completed work, customer relationships, property listings, or business-profile eligibility. A branded illustration is not an authentic portfolio project or a photograph of our warehouse. Use contextual labels where the placement would otherwise mislead.

For approved uniform or vehicle branding, use the actual logo accurately. Respect perspective, fabric, folds, seams, lighting, size, and crop. Use the approved one-colour variant when appropriate. Reject malformed lettering, improvised logos, unnatural placements, implausible machinery, unsafe work practices, and inconsistent protective equipment. Correct unusable images rather than hiding defects with a crop.

Keep headings, important claims, navigation, and interface text in accessible HTML rather than baked into images. Logo graphics must have an accessible brand name or appropriate alt text. Provide useful alt text for other meaningful images, empty alt text for decorative images, focal points for responsive crops, image dimensions, responsive sources, and optimized formats. Prioritize the principal above-the-fold image and lazy-load appropriate lower-page media.

Keep editable originals and a media record identifying source, rights/provenance, intended page, and whether the asset is illustrative. Preserve required AI-image metadata when a platform or product feed requires it. Do not strip provenance to disguise an image's origin.

### Owner-supplied video

Inspect the footage, duration, orientation, audio and colour before choosing a useful placement. A supplied clip is not proof that the business performed the work shown. Preserve the original and create an optimized web derivative and poster with reserved dimensions. Choose resolution and compression for the displayed size, verify colour on target browsers, and convert HDR to SDR when compatibility requires it. Use a broadly supported format and appropriate progressive-download preparation, such as MP4 fast-start, when suitable; avoid shipping a large camera original by default.

Prefer user-initiated playback with accessible controls, inline mobile playback and a poster; use a conservative preload hint such as `none` for lower-page clips. Preserve meaningful audio and provide captions, transcripts or descriptions as the content requires. A silent ambient edit can be appropriate, but record that choice rather than silently removing useful speech. Exclude unnecessary private camera/location metadata from public derivatives while retaining provenance records and required disclosures.

Test real playback, advancing time, pause, controls, layout and loading on the final host. A valid asset URL or HTTP 200 response does not prove playback, seeking or range-request support. Record file size, dimensions, duration, audio treatment and the live asset URL in the media record.

## 8. PHASE 1C — CONVERSION AND ENQUIRY DELIVERY

Build the complete main-action journey in both languages. Telephone links use a valid international number; email links use the correct brand inbox. Avoid excessive popups and unnecessary required fields.

Keep the initial form concise. Capture name, a reliable contact method, location, offering/inquiry type, scope, and timing. Request company, facility, quantities, dates, dimensions, site access, attachments, or other details only where they help qualify that particular enquiry. Budget can be optional.

Carry the selected service, product family or rental package into the correct request type and fields, including through a language switch where practical. Use stable identifiers and safely encoded, validated public context; never put personal form data in URL parameters. Do not silently default an installation or rental request to an unrelated service such as repair. When the request type changes, preserve compatible entered values, clear incompatible selections and submit only relevant fields. Test representative entry links, type changes, back navigation and EN/FR switching.

Adapt intake to the service. Equipment sales need application and specifications; rentals need dates and fulfillment; high dusting needs height/surfaces/access; moving needs origin/destination and handling; furniture installation needs systems, quantities, plans, and receiving status. Do not use a generic relocation form for every installation enquiry.

Implement accessible labels, autocomplete, clear required/optional fields, client and server validation, useful error messages, submission progress, duplicate-submission handling, success/failure states, and a clear privacy explanation. Preserve entered data on recoverable errors. Do not require marketing opt-in to request a quote. Use separate optional marketing consent where applicable.

Use a configured service or compatible backend for delivery. Keep credentials server-side and out of source control. Validate bot-verification tokens on the server. Check the actual capabilities of any managed form service before relying on it.

Test an authorized clearly labelled submission end to end: the user interface, endpoint response, processing/provider evidence, and receipt in the destination inbox where access allows. Distinguish accepted, queued, saved, notification delivered, and inbox receipt when those states can be observed. An HTTP success response alone does not establish inbox delivery. State exactly what was verified.

Never show a fake success state or silently discard a lead. If the endpoint is unavailable, provide a truthful fallback using functioning contact methods and report the integration as blocked. Do not launch an active-looking form that cannot process enquiries.

Avoid collecting sensitive health, legal, financial, employee, identity, or accessibility information through a general enquiry form. Keep private submissions, attachments, and generated customer outputs out of public pages, URL parameters, analytics, and GitHub. Configure retention and access proportional to the data collected.

## 11. PHASE 2B — NATIONAL COVERAGE WITHOUT THIN LOCATION PAGES

Design a national content architecture from launch. Plan all ten provinces and three territories, major cities, metropolitan areas, and surrounding communities relevant to the business. This is a coverage plan, not permission to publish every planned URL.

Prioritize regions by actual delivery feasibility, buyer relevance, and useful content. Use national and regional hubs to explain coverage honestly where city detail is not yet justified. Do not automatically produce the full city × service × industry × language combination.

Apply this project's publication gate to each geographic page:

1. Identify the specific visitor need and confirm what the business can actually offer there.
2. Add verified, decision-useful regional information: for example, delivery/dispatch arrangements, genuine local scope, access/logistical constraints, jurisdiction-specific requirements, or accurate service contacts for an information resource.
3. Make the page useful on its own, in a coherent browsable hierarchy, with an appropriate action.
4. Review factual claims, French equivalence, overlaps with adjacent pages, internal links, media context, and publication/indexing status.

Different wording, a new city image, a population figure, local landmarks, or replacing a city name does not establish local value. These are our publishing safeguards; Google does not prescribe this particular checklist or a minimum word/page count.

Maintain a coverage record: region, service/subject availability, evidence, useful local detail, EN/FR routes, review status, and indexability. Keep unfinished pages unpublished. Consolidate overlapping or thin pages into stronger hubs. If a utility page must be public but is not intended for discovery, handle indexing as described in Section 14.

Never invent branches, addresses, local teams, projects, municipal endorsements, response times, or location schema. Do not automatically geotarget a national brand as physically located in every city.

## 12. FULL ENGLISH AND QUÉBEC FRENCH FROM LAUNCH

Launch complete Canadian English and natural Canadian French appropriate for Québec. French is a first-class customer experience. It must not be deferred to a later growth phase without an explicit project override.

Translate body content, menus, buttons, forms, validation/errors, confirmations, notifications, policy text, metadata, accessibility labels, downloads intended for both audiences, and meaningful text in graphics. Preserve brand names and technical terms where appropriate. Use a shared terminology record and check French fit in the actual design.

Use stable locale routes, normally /en/ and /fr/, with a route-equivalence map and localized slugs where useful. Switching language should open the corresponding page, not send every visitor to the homepage. Remember a chosen language where appropriate; do not force IP-based redirects or prevent visitors from choosing.

Implement correct document language, fully qualified reciprocal en-CA/fr-CA hreflang alternates including self references, and a sensible x-default where applicable. Each genuine translated page normally has its own self-canonical URL. Do not canonicalize all French pages to English. Generate alternates only for real equivalent pages that exist.

Test the entire French enquiry journey and header/mobile layout, including catalogue states, package choices, media descriptions and error messages. Check narrow mobile widths such as 320 and 390 CSS px, tablet navigation and long labels without clipping or horizontal scrolling. A language switch should retain the visitor's page and safe offering context. Treat an untranslated required interaction as a launch defect. Do not claim professional linguistic review if none occurred; identify any material specialist-language review still needed.

## 13. SEARCH DISCOVERY, STRUCTURED DATA, AND ANSWERS

Build search engine optimization (SEO) around helpful crawlable content and clear page purposes. Treat answer engine optimization (AEO) as clear, well-supported publishing, not a separate collection of hidden tricks.

- Map each indexable page to a real visitor intent. Write accurate unique titles, useful descriptions, one clear primary heading, and a logical heading structure.
- Render essential content and real links in HTML. Avoid requiring interaction or client JavaScript for a crawler to discover the core offering.
- Use descriptive internal links, breadcrumbs, and related content that help people navigate. Audit the intended destination, not just whether a link returns a successful status.
- Establish one preferred HTTPS hostname and consistent URL rules. Implement real redirects when moving URLs. Avoid redirect chains, soft 404s, and routing every invalid path to a successful homepage.
- Generate XML sitemaps from approved, canonical, indexable URLs only. Include truthful modification dates. Exclude private pages, drafts, redirects, errors, filtered duplicates, and utility pages not meant for discovery.
- Validate canonical URLs, locale alternates, social metadata, icons, robots controls, and rendered page content on the actual deployment host.

Use JSON-LD only for relevant visible facts. Appropriate types may include Organization, WebSite, Service, BreadcrumbList, Product, or Article. Select types based on the actual entity and page. LocalBusiness markup must not manufacture a physical presence. Do not invent required fields to satisfy a validator. Do not add fictitious reviews, ratings, prices, authors, credentials, events, or offers. Check current feature-specific eligibility before adding FAQ or other rich-result markup; successful validation does not guarantee a rich result.

Write concise direct answers where useful, support technical/regulated claims with primary sources, identify real authors/reviewers where available, and show actual review dates. Do not change dates just to look fresh. No promised rankings, indexing, rich results, AI citations, or AI answer inclusion. Google does not require special AI files or special schema for its AI Search features; do not make llms.txt, hidden instructions, or speculative AI markup a launch requirement.

## 14. GOOGLE ABUSE PREVENTION AND PUBLICATION CONTROLS

Read the current official guidance before launch:

- [Prevent user-generated spam](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/prevent-abuse)
- [Google Search spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- [Helpful, reliable content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- [Generative AI content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content)
- [Noindex implementation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/block-indexing)

The first page chiefly concerns public contributions and generated/shared pages. Apply those controls when those features exist. Also assess the separate search risks of low-value scaled publishing and doorway sites/pages. Multiple brands, reusable templates, AI assistance, and useful location pages are not prohibited by themselves; these project safeguards address low-value or misleading implementations.

### Brand and content safeguards

Give every launched brand independent customer value and a real offering or information purpose. Reusable components are acceptable; domain/colour/keyword swaps are insufficient differentiation. Do not create substantially similar sites or city pages merely to funnel the same search intent elsewhere, use portfolio-wide keyword footer links, buy links for ranking, hide keyword text, or repurpose expired domains mainly to exploit old ranking signals. Publish for customers, not page counts. AI-assisted drafting is acceptable; review factual accuracy, originality, metadata, French, and media before publication. Human review is required where specialist judgment is material, and must not be falsely claimed.

### Public contributions and form abuse

Ordinary quote submissions remain private and never create public pages. If comments, profiles, listings, reviews, uploads, or shareable generated pages are actually required, implement an abuse policy/report route, moderation, suspicious-activity controls, and appropriate ugc/nofollow link treatment. New unreviewed contributions must not be automatically indexable. Add proportionate rate/volume limits, server validation, and accessible bot controls to public submission flows. Monitor suspicious URL growth, irrelevant search queries, traffic spikes, and Search Console security/manual-action alerts. Do not build a forum or registration system just to implement this section.

### Indexing and privacy are separate controls

Use authentication/authorization for private data. For accessible utility, internal-search, share, or generated-output pages not intended for search discovery, use a proper noindex meta tag or X-Robots-Tag and omit them from sitemaps. A crawler must be able to fetch a page to see noindex; do not rely on a robots.txt block to deliver that instruction. Robots rules and noindex do not secure confidential information. Keep development previews private where supported; otherwise apply deliberate preview indexing controls and verify production is not accidentally excluded.

### Real business representation

Use accurate business identity, contact, and location/service-area information. Do not manufacture storefront, warehouse, or team evidence for Google Business Profile or imply that each domain qualifies for a separate profile. Follow [Google's business representation guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) when a profile is in scope. Website launch alone does not establish profile eligibility.

These safeguards reduce avoidable risk; they are not a guarantee of Google policy approval or search inclusion.

## 15. TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE, SECURITY, AND PERFORMANCE

Fit the implementation to the actual hosting environment. Preserve a sound existing framework, package manager, and conventions. For a new content/lead site, prefer a maintainable static-first or server-rendered approach with structured content and limited client JavaScript. Choose a stack for the job; do not require Next.js, a single-file generator, Vercel, Supabase, or a database universally.

For WHC hosting, establish the actual plan and runtime support before choosing server dependencies. A static build needs a supported external or compatible server endpoint for forms. Do not assume a cPanel account runs Node services or serverless functions. Document build output, clean URL rules, redirects, asset paths, secrets, HTTPS, and upload/deploy procedures.

For OpenAI Sites, follow the current platform workflow and existing project configuration. Reuse the exact existing project ID; never create a duplicate Site to bypass a source/recovery problem. Track the deployed source revision and project/domain mapping. Do not embed obsolete tool commands in operating instructions; use current supported capabilities.

Keep content structured with explicit draft/published and indexable states. Generate routes, navigation, sitemaps, and locale maps from reliable records rather than scattered duplicated strings. Use a single authoritative contact/configuration record per brand and environment.

Apply proportionate security: server-side validation, output escaping, request/body limits, authorization where needed, restricted administrative access, secret handling, safe dependencies, and supported security headers. For cookie-authenticated state changes, add appropriate cross-site request protections. Frontend validation and honeypots alone are insufficient abuse controls.

For document/photo uploads, allow only needed file types and sizes, validate content appropriately, use safe storage names, keep files private and non-executable, and apply scanning or a supported secure-upload provider where required. If secure uploads cannot be supported, offer a safe alternative rather than publishing an open upload endpoint. Keep sensitive payloads out of logs.

Use HTTPS, optimize principal assets, reserve image space, avoid unnecessary scripts/fonts, and prevent avoidable layout shift. Measure representative production pages on mobile and desktop with available tools. Check current Core Web Vitals guidance; report the test environment and distinguish laboratory results from real-user field data. Do not fabricate scores or claim a passing field assessment for a new site with no field data.

Implement Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 AA as the target: semantic structure, keyboard operation, visible focus, skip links, contrast, zoom/reflow, meaningful labels, accessible errors, appropriate target sizes, and reduced motion. Use automated checks plus manual interaction checks. Do not label the website certified or fully compliant based on an automated score.

Privacy notices must describe the actual data flow and tools. Load analytics/marketing tools only as appropriate to the approved setup and applicable consent requirements. Track useful events without sending sensitive enquiry content. Never invent analytics IDs, CRM connections, privacy guarantees, or legal compliance claims.
